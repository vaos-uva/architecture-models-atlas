# KNIGA Content Style Guide

This guide distills the attached OZCO practicum writing notes for architecture education and research content. It governs explanatory copy, not normative source claims.

## Audience and voice

Write for experienced IT professionals moving toward solution-architect work. Assume practical development and team experience. Do not explain basic terms mechanically; explain why a decision matters under a specific constraint.

Use a senior-practitioner voice: direct, collaborative, respectful, and concrete. Prefer active sentences of roughly 15–25 words. Keep paragraphs short. In Russian-language learning content, address the reader informally («ты») and keep technical English terms when they are the clearest term.

## Architecture explanations

Connect every architecture concept to a business requirement, technical constraint, stakeholder concern, or delivery decision. Present architecture as a set of trade-offs, not as an absolute prescription.

For a case study, show the constraints that shaped the decision, the alternatives considered, and why they were rejected. For metrics, state the decision each metric informs and distinguish technical, product, business, team, and project measures.

For model content, use the pattern **definition → example → boundary**. State whether a claim is normative, explanatory, derived, or atlas synthesis. Do not turn a secondary explainer into a standard or imply equivalence between frameworks with different purposes.

## Preferred language

Prefer concrete verbs such as *define*, *compare*, *trace*, *validate*, *calculate*, and *decide*. Avoid bureaucratic filler, passive academic phrasing, marketing claims, and unsupported generalizations.

Use tables when a real comparison benefits from aligned columns. Keep code and CLI examples minimal and purposeful. For architecture sources, preserve the source's terminology and add a plain-language explanation beside it rather than silently rewriting the meaning.

## Source and trust rules

Attach source URLs to the data or claim they support. Keep official specifications ahead of secondary explainers. Label interpretive bridges explicitly, especially when borrowing concepts from philosophy or mathematics to explain architecture modeling.
